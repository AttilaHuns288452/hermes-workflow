# Worked Example — Group PM conflict (Bernadette, Aug 2026)

Full arc of one coaching engagement, showing the protocol in action. Quotes are from the actual GC thread.

## Situation

Attila (PM) of an 8-person university software project group sent a GC message: meetings only when needed (major decisions/features/integration/issues), he assigns tasks, reviews work, gives feedback via F2F/Messenger, wants to maximize dev time ("may Software Engineering pa tayo"), ends with "HAHAHA baka awayin niyo ako…" and "In short: Maximize efficiency and results."

Bernadette replied: "if naiintindihan mo talaga yung part mo as a pm i think u should read again the announcements na meron si louie sa gc… behind na tau sa kanila kasi may mga daily schedules/daily meetings na sila… kung ang stand mo is pag may proposal kana gagawa or iaapply un, i think indi sha applicable… if u want, i can help u about it… di pwede petiks petiks and wag mo gawin reason na may soft eng din tau…"

## Evidence moves that mattered

1. **The paraphrase test.** Her "kung ang stand mo is pag may proposal kana gagawa or iaapply un" accurately restates his position → P(she misunderstood) ≈ 0.05. It was disagreement, not comprehension failure. This single finding reframed the whole engagement and prevented a manifesto re-send.
2. **Omission check on the user's own summary.** His retelling of the professor's announcement omitted the daily-meeting requirement — the one fact making her reading rational. Omission = evidence; flag it.
3. **Axis mismatch.** She argued cadence (daily syncs); he rebutted deliverables (schedule + interviews done). Both right, both unheard. ("sa daily meetings naman na sinasabi ni louie niraraise ko boi.")
4. **Correlation-as-causation.** "May daily meetings sila → behind tayo" is her weakest link; name it, concede the rest (GC chaos was a valid uncontested observation; "same prof lang yan" was valid on merits — the soft-eng sentence was excuse-SHAPED even with clean intent).
5. **Bias credited where real.** She assumed failure instead of asking ("read again the announcements"), never updated her prior, framed character not behavior ("petiks") — but she also de-escalated three times ("gowch puh i trust u," "im not mad okay?"). Both sides' FAE/confirmation bias named, not just hers.
6. **Missing evidence requested.** "What did she reply to your DM?" — her actual reply ("sige tol, help and update n lang kita… wish ko lang sana makinig ka huhu… gl satin tol") then became the next analysis object; warm message, one soft phrase, user read it as an insult → rumination intervention.

## Reply-draft pattern that fit

Bridge reply: acknowledge valid core ("gets ko, required yung daily syncs") + confirm cadence explicitly + show receipts (pin schedule/interviews) + accept her offer with a role (tracker owner). Never re-send the manifesto. Later DM (user's own, better than the draft): ask her to forward exact announcement texts — primary source over summary, gives her a role, fact-finds the requirement. It worked: exact text was pasted next day.

## Rumination arc (named, in order)

she misunderstood → she's biased → she called my sentence an excuse → social proof → "sana makinig ka" implies I don't listen → task assigned to me is shameful → fear of future reputation damage → "am I overthinking?" Each was partly valid; the aggregate function was collecting evidence against a de-escalated opponent. Break: evidence trail of her five peace behaviors + "the conflict is over on her side; the remaining conflict is internal" + highest-EV action unchanged in N messages. Resolution came from the user himself: "if she wants an extra requirement, I'll just do it and not make a fuss" — recorded as first positive "changes opinion with evidence" data point in user_context.md audit.

## Transferable one-liners

- "If it isn't shared, from their perspective it didn't happen."
- "Comprehension fixes with explanation; priors fix with evidence."
- "You can't paraphrase someone's position accurately and claim you misunderstood it."
- "A message can be clear about intent and incomplete about context at the same time."
- "Arguing well does not build reputation. Delivering does."
