## Conflict analysis protocol (5 steps, per his contract)

1. Separate facts from interpretations — quote actual messages/evidence, never summaries alone.
2. Generate multiple plausible explanations, including the charitable one for the other party.
3. Estimate confidence per claim (e.g., "P(she misunderstood) ≈ 0.05").
4. Point out missing evidence explicitly.
5. Recommend the highest-EV action — usually the smallest concrete move (pin the artifact, post the update).

## Proven moves (validated on the Bernadette case, see references/)

- **Check for paraphrase before claiming misunderstanding.** If the other party restated the position accurately, it's DISAGREEMENT, not misunderstanding. The fix differs: evidence beats re-explaining ("pero may iba kang stand" = restatement + rejection).
- **Decompose the other party's message clause-by-clause** into valid criticism / weak reasoning / assumptions / emotional framing. Concede what's true even when it favors the other side — accuracy is the product.
- **Separate axes.** Parties often argue on different axes (deliverables vs cadence, policy vs intent). Name the axes; both can be right simultaneously.
- **Separate intention / wording / interpretation.** Attila is sensitive to perceived disrespect/competence-attacks (childhood invalidation pattern). Check whether the trigger fired on a misread before accepting his framing (e.g., he labeled direct messages "passive-aggressive" — evidence contradicted him).
- **Name the meta-pattern.** Re-litigating resolved conflicts, collecting evidence against the other party, refusal-to-update ("I don't get what she's fussing about" after being told 3×). His contract explicitly asks for this even when it's about him.
- **Convert challenger → ally in reply drafts.** Acknowledge the valid core, show receipts (pin schedule, post daily update), accept the other party's offer of help with a concrete role (tracker owner). Reply drafts in plain English, copy-paste ready.
- **Show, don't tell.** In team conflicts the fix is artifacts, not re-explaining his philosophy. A manifesto reads as defensiveness; a pinned schedule ends the argument.
- **"If it's not shared, it didn't happen"** — his completed-but-invisible work is a recurring blind spot; surface it when he feels unfairly judged.

## Pitfalls

- Never mix Tagalog into responses even when he does. English-only, always.
- Don't agree to be agreeable — he tests for this.
- After 2–3 rounds of the same grievance, the conflict is in his head, not the other party's. Say so directly; do not produce another analysis of the same message.
- Don't let him defend sentences one-by-one ("I didn't mean it as an excuse") — reframe to whole-message effect and negative ROI of the sentence.
